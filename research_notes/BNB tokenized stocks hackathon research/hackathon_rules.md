### Takeaway

Dry-run with the Transaction API while you build, then demo with small live amounts."** — [BNB Chain Blog: BNB Hack: Tokenized Stocks Edition, with Binance Web3 Wallet](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/bnb-hack-tokenized-stocks-edition-with-binance-web3-wallet)

- A WebSearch synthesis (not a primary-source direct quote) characterized enforcement as: "The hackathon explicitly requires spot-only trading, with perpetuals excluded," and separately noted community-built projects self-describing compliance, e.g. one project "enforces strictly spot-only execution on BSC Mainnet, with perpetuals, margin, and leverage rejected at the type level" (a submission's own code-level design choice, not an official rule quote) and another "specifies BSC mainnet spot only" — these are third-party hackathon submission repos (e.g., https://github.com/JimmyOgb/stockpilot, https://github.com/Adeyemir/bellproof), not official rule text, and should not be read as the organizer's own words. The hackathon landing page states "Full terms sit on the hackathon page" (i.e., https://www.bnbchain.org/en/hackathons/tokenized-stocks itself is presented as the full-terms document; there is no separate expanded legal/FAQ document) — [per KuCoin News secondary summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/trends/BNB/6aade4e562cf370007430efd), corroborated by the absence of any linked secondary rules document in the blog post or hackathon page fetches.

### Inferences
- The rule as written names exactly one excluded product category by name — "perps" (perpetual futures/perpetual swaps) — and does not use the words "leverage," "margin," "derivative," "short," "option," "synthetic," or "insurance" anywhere in the primary source text retrieved. This is a narrow, product-name-based exclusion rather than a broad principle-based exclusion.
- "BSC mainnet only" is a separate, independent constraint (deployment chain) bundled into the same sentence as the trading-product constraint, but they are logically distinct requirements.

### Gaps
- No official FAQ, clarification thread, or Discord Q&A addressing edge cases (e.g., options, structured notes, parametric payouts, prediction markets, or insurance-style products) could be found. This is the central gap for the report's key determination below.

## Key Question 12 (Central Determination): Is a fixed-term, pre-funded, non-leveraged downside-protection/insurance mechanism CLEARLY ALLOWED, CLEARLY BANNED, or AMBIGUOUS under the spot-only rule?

### Takeaway
**AMBIGUOUS.** The only official rule text found is the single sentence **"Spot only. Perps are out. BSC mainnet only."** — [BNB Chain Blog](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/bnb-hack-tokenized-stocks-edition-with-binance-web3-wallet), reconfirmed on the [hackathon page](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/hackathons/tokenized-stocks). This sentence names exactly one excluded product type by name ("perps" = perpetual futures/swaps) and does not use the words "leverage," "margin," "derivative," "option," "short," "synthetic," "structured product," or "insurance" anywhere in any primary source retrieved. No FAQ, clarification, or Q&A thread addressing parametric-insurance-style or contingent-payout products could be found. This is an explicit gap, not a basis for assuming permission either way.

### Cited Findings
- Exact rule text: **"Spot only. Perps are out. BSC mainnet only."** — [BNB Chain Blog](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/bnb-hack-tokenized-stocks-edition-with-binance-web3-wallet); reconfirmed verbatim: "Trading Constraints: 'Spot only. Perps are out.'" — [BNB Chain hackathon page](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/hackathons/tokenized-stocks)
- No broader/generalized "no derivatives of any kind" language was found verbatim in either direct fetch of the primary sources. The word "derivatives" only appeared in a third-party summarization tool's own paraphrase (see Key Question 11), not in BNB Chain's original text.
- At least two hackathon participant teams' own submissions interpreted "spot only" broadly, on their own initiative, to also exclude margin and leverage explicitly at the code/type level (e.g., a project described as enforcing "strictly spot-only execution on BSC Mainnet, with perpetuals, margin, and leverage rejected at the type level" — per WebSearch synthesis of GitHub repo https://github.com/JimmyOgb/stockpilot content) — this shows participant-side caution/self-interpretation, not an official ruling, and does not resolve whether a fixed-payout contingent product (which has no margin/leverage but does have a contingent/triggered payout) would pass or fail screening.
- No hackathon submission, official communication, or third-party source found that specifically discusses an insurance-style, parametric, premium-for-fixed-payout product in the context of this hackathon's rules.

### Reasoning
Arguments that such a mechanism would likely be **permitted** under the literal rule as written:
1. The rule excludes "perps" by name. Perpetual futures/swaps are characterized by margin requirements, leverage, funding-rate mechanics, and potentially open-ended liability (a trader can be liquidated for more than initial capital in some designs). The described mechanism — fixed premium paid up front into a pre-funded pool, fixed payout on a trigger before a fixed expiry, no margin, no leverage, no short position, no open-ended liability — affirmatively lacks every one of the risk characteristics that make perpetuals a target of concern (e.g., insolvency risk, systemic leverage risk, regulatory risk around margin trading). A narrow reading of "Spot only. Perps are out." as shorthand for "no margin/leveraged trading products" would not obviously capture this mechanism.
2. The rule is phrased as a short, informal one-liner in a blog post, consistent with excluding the single most obvious/regulator-sensitive category (leveraged perpetuals trading) rather than laying out a comprehensive product taxonomy.

Arguments that such a mechanism could still be considered **banned or excluded** under a stricter reading:
1. "Spot only" can be read literally as requiring that the tokenized-stock leg of any submission be a literal spot purchase/holding/sale of the underlying asset — with no payoff structure that is contingent on, or derived from, a triggering condition. A parametric/insurance-style payout is, in traditional-finance terms, a contingent claim / derivative instrument (its payout derives from an external condition rather than being the direct spot transfer of the asset itself), even though it carries none of the leverage/margin risk that "perps" carry. Screeners applying a strict "is this a derivative, yes/no" test (rather than a "does this have leverage/margin, yes/no" test) could disqualify it at the "completeness and eligibility" screening stage, which the source explicitly says happens **before** the weighted judging criteria are applied — [per WebFetch synthesis, BNB Chain Blog](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/bnb-hack-tokenized-stocks-edition-with-binance-web3-wallet).
2. No participant-visible FAQ or organizer clarification exists to resolve which reading is correct, and organizers/screeners retain discretion.

### Inferences
- Given the rule's plain text targets "perps" specifically and does not mention derivatives, options, or contingent payouts generally, a narrow reading would likely treat a non-leveraged, non-margined, pre-funded fixed-payout product as outside the literal exclusion. However, this is an inference from the absence of broader language, not a confirmed organizer position — it should not be treated as a green light.
- A team pursuing this concept should consider (a) directly asking the organizers (e.g., via the hackathon's official Discord/contact channel referenced on the hackathon page, not found/accessible in this research session) for an explicit ruling before building, and (b) architecting the submission so the tokenized-stock spot leg (e.g., actually holding/trading bStocks/Ondo/xStocks tokens) is clearly the "central" product per the required-protocol rule, with the insurance/payout mechanism framed as a wrapper/feature around that spot position rather than as a standalone derivative product — this could strengthen a "spot-only compliant" argument, though this is a strategic suggestion, not a rule-derived certainty.

### Gaps
- **No explicit rule text, FAQ, or organizer clarification addresses parametric-insurance-style, fixed-payout, or contingent-claim products specifically.** This is the central open ambiguity and could not be resolved with the sources available in this research session (no DoraHacks page, no FAQ/GitBook, no accessible Discord archive, no accessible X/Twitter thread with organizer Q&A were found).
- Could not determine who specifically screens submissions for eligibility (e.g., automated check vs. human reviewer) or what discretion they exercise in borderline cases.
- Recommend explicit outreach to hackathon organizers for a ruling before committing significant build time to this concept, given the stakes of a screening-stage disqualification (which occurs before any of the four weighted judging criteria are even applied).
