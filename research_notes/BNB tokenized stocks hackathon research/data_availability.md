# Data Availability for a BSC Tokenized-Stock Weekend/Reference-Price Product

## Does Chainlink provide equity/stock price feeds on BNB Smart Chain? Which tickers, and what are the exact feeds?

### Takeaway
Yes. Chainlink runs standard "Equity" category price feeds for at least AAPL/USD, TSLA/USD, and NVDA/USD on BNB Chain (BSC) mainnet, confirmed live on data.chain.link. However, these appear to be conventional Chainlink Price Feeds (deviation + heartbeat triggered against real stock-market pricing), not the newer "24/5 Tokenized Equity Feeds" product line, which Chainlink's own docs currently scope to Base mainnet only (for Coinbase/Ondo/Robinhood token issuers). This distinction matters: standard equity feeds on BSC are documented elsewhere to go stale over weekends/holidays (see staleness section) because the underlying stock market itself is closed and the feed simply stops updating — it is not designed as a 24/5 "wrapper price" feed.

### Cited Findings
- Chainlink lists a **TSLA / USD Price Feed** on "BSC / mainnet" at `https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/tsla-usd` — [Source](https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/tsla-usd)
- Chainlink lists an **AAPL / USD Price Feed** on "BSC / mainnet" at `https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/aapl-usd` — [Source](https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/aapl-usd)
- Chainlink lists an **NVDA / USD Price Feed** on "BSC / mainnet" at `https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/nvda-usd`, with contract address beginning `0xea5c...99B8` per search indexing (exact full address not independently confirmed via a direct fetch — data.chain.link blocked direct WebFetch with HTTP 403, so this address should be re-verified directly on data.chain.link or BscScan before being hard-coded into a contract) — [Source](https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/nvda-usd)
- Chainlink's official "Tokenized Equity Feeds" documentation (the 24/5, issuer-specific feed product covering Ondo Finance, Robinhood, and Coinbase token designs) explicitly scopes availability to **Base Mainnet** as of the docs consulted; it does not list BNB Chain as a network for this specific product line — [Source](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/tokenized-equity-feeds)
- Chainlink's separate, newer **"24/5 U.S. Equities Streams"** product (a pull-based Data Streams product providing Regular/Extended/Overnight-hours feeds with market-status flags and staleness indicators) is reported as already integrated with **40+ blockchains** via the Chainlink Data Standard, with named integrators including Lighter, BitMEX, ApeX, HelloTrade, Decibel, Monaco, Opinion Labs, and Orderly Network — BSC is not explicitly named among these in the sources found — [Source](https://blog.chain.link/chainlink-24-5-us-equities-streams/); [Source](https://docs.chain.link/data-streams/rwa-streams/24-5-us-equities-user-guide)
- A third-party engineering write-up analyzing the (non-BSC, but architecturally identical) Chainlink AAPL/USD standard price feed found: "The feeds are deviation-triggered, and the 86,400 second heartbeat essentially never fires... a weekend freezes the feed for 48 to 58 hours; Labor Day stretched that past 81 hours... The Friday stop is not the closing bell but the last half-percent move, which on one sample was five and a half hours before the close" — [Source](https://github.com/moonwell-fi/mamo-contracts/pull/106)
- Chainlink also confirms standard BNB Chain mainnet crypto feeds exist and are well-documented (BNB/USD, BTC/USD, LINK/BNB, etc.) via `data.chain.link` and BscScan, establishing that Chainlink's oracle infrastructure is actively deployed and maintained on BSC generally — [Source](https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/bnb-usd); [Source](https://bscscan.com/address/0x0567F2323251f0Aab15c8dFb1967E4e8A7D42aeE)

### Inferences
- A hackathon team CAN pull a real, live, on-chain Chainlink AAPL/USD, TSLA/USD, or NVDA/USD price on BSC mainnet today by calling `latestRoundData()` on the documented feed address — this is a genuine on-chain reference price, not a mock.
- However, because these are standard (not 24/5) equity feeds, the price will very likely **stop updating from Friday's last big move through Monday's market open** — which is actually a *feature* for this specific product's "Friday close price" and "reference price" needs, but a problem if the team wants a continuously fresh weekend price. The `updatedAt` field returned by `latestRoundData()` combined with the last stored `answer` is effectively already a "Friday close" proxy plus a staleness signal for free — no extra product needed for that specific requirement.
- The "24/5" always-on equity data (better for a live weekend/overnight signal) exists as a Chainlink product but its confirmed public integrations are elsewhere (Base, and various perp DEXs); its presence on BSC specifically was not found in public sources and should be treated as unconfirmed/likely absent for now.

### Gaps
- Full BSC-mainnet feed addresses for TSLA/USD, AAPL/USD, and NVDA/USD could not be independently confirmed byte-for-byte via direct page fetch (data.chain.link returned HTTP 403 to the automated fetch tool used in this research); a team should visit `https://data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/aapl-usd` etc. directly in a browser or via the Chainlink feed registry JSON to get exact checksummed addresses, decimals, heartbeat, and deviation threshold before building.
- No comprehensive list of *every* equity ticker Chainlink supports on BSC was retrievable (only AAPL, TSLA, NVDA were confirmed); there may be more (e.g., MSFT, GOOGL, SPY) — team should filter `data.chain.link/feeds?categories=Equity` and select network "BNB Chain" directly.
- Could not confirm whether Chainlink's 24/5 U.S. Equities Streams product is deployable/queryable on BSC at all (pull-oracle streams are typically chain-agnostic if a receiver contract is deployed, but no explicit BSC deployment or verifier contract address was found in this research).

### Inferences

This is realistic for a hackathon (well-documented SDKs exist) but is an active integration step, not a passive read.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the exact, byte-perfect BNB Chain Pyth contract address independently (search-engine excerpt only); team should confirm at `https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/contract-addresses/evm` directly.
- Could not confirm Pyth's equity feeds' update cadence/market-hours behavior (i.e., whether Pyth's TSLA/AAPL feeds also go stale over the weekend the same way Chainlink's standard equity feeds do, or whether Pyth aggregates from sources that produce a different/continuous synthetic price). This should be checked directly on the Pyth price feed page (confidence interval and "last updated" shown live) before assuming continuous weekend freshness.

## Is there a free/on-chain source for "is NYSE/NASDAQ open right now," or must this be computed from calendar + hours?

### Takeaway
There is no widely-used free, dedicated, always-live "market open/closed" on-chain oracle; the realistic path is either (a) a free/freemium REST API from a market-data vendor (Alpaca's `/v2/clock`, Finnhub's `/stock/market-status`, or Polygon.io's market-status endpoint), or (b) computing it yourself from NYSE's official published hours + holiday calendar, which is straightforward and free but requires handling early-close days and DST correctly.

### Cited Findings
- **Alpaca Markets** exposes a free **Clock API** — `GET /v2/clock` — that "serves the current market timestamp, whether or not the market is currently open, as well as the times of the next market open and close." Alpaca's Data/Trading API free plan is the default plan on both paper and live accounts — [Source](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/legacyclock); [Source](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.4.2/reference/querymarketclock)
- **Finnhub** offers a `market_status` endpoint that "gets current market status for global exchanges (whether exchanges are open or close)," parameterized by exchange; however, "free Finnhub plans only support US; other exchanges are premium-gated" — for a US-only use case (NYSE/NASDAQ), this fits the free tier. Finnhub's free tier generally allows 60 calls/minute — [Source](https://finnhub.io/); [Source](https://freeapi.watch/finnhub/)
- **Polygon.io** has a market-status endpoint that can return values like "open," "closed," or "extended-hours"; Polygon's free API tier is rate-limited to roughly 5 requests/minute — [Source](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/clock-api-and-extended-hours/118)
- **Financial Modeling Prep** offers a "Global Exchange Market Hours API" tracking when NYSE/NASDAQ are open, with timezone and real-time status — pricing tier not confirmed as free vs paid in the sources found — [Source](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/stable/exchange-market-hours)
- The **official NYSE holiday/hours calendar** is published directly by NYSE at `nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars`, and lists 2026 holidays (New Year's Day, MLK Day Jan 19, Washington's Birthday Feb 16, Good Friday Apr 3, Memorial Day May 25, Juneteenth Jun 19, Independence Day observed Jul 3, Labor Day Sep 7, Thanksgiving Nov 26, Christmas Dec 25) plus 1:00pm early closes on Nov 27, 2026 and Dec 24, 2026; regular hours are 9:30am–4:00pm ET Mon–Fri — [Source](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars); [Source](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/ICE_NYSE_2026_Yearly_Trading_Calendar.pdf)
- A third-party "Market Status API" (built by an independent developer, distributed on Apify) is described as "a lightweight, dependency-free market status API with no scraping or external calls, computed from embedded exchange calendars," covering NYSE, LSE, JPX — this is not an official exchange product and its reliability/maintenance status as a long-term dependency is unverified — [Source](https://apify.com/ichigowa/market-status-api)

### Inferences
- For a hackathon MVP, the most reliable "is the market open" answer is realistically a **self-computed check** against NYSE's official hours (9:30am–4:00pm ET, Mon–Fri) plus the official 2026 holiday list, since this is free, has zero external dependency/rate-limit risk, and the holiday list changes predictably and rarely (NYSE publishes 3 years out). A live API (Alpaca's free `/v2/clock`, or Finnhub's free US market-status endpoint) is a reasonable *supplement/cross-check* on top of that, not a replacement, given free-tier rate limits.
- No source found suggests an on-chain, decentralized-oracle-native "is NYSE open" boolean feed exists on any chain (Chainlink/Pyth's equity feeds encode market-session info indirectly via staleness, not via an explicit boolean flag on standard feeds — though Chainlink's *newer* 24/5 Streams product does claim to deliver "market-status flags" as part of its payload, which would be the closest on-chain-native answer, but is not confirmed available on BSC).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm Financial Modeling Prep's or Marketstack's exact free-tier pricing/limits for a market-hours-specific endpoint (marketstack.com itself was not directly queried in this research due to tool budget; team should check pricing directly at marketstack.com and financialmodelingprep.com before relying on either).
- Could not confirm whether Chainlink's 24/5 Streams "market-status flag" is queryable on BSC specifically (see oracle section above).

## Free/low-cost APIs for real historical daily stock closes (Friday close, Monday reopen) for TSLA/AAPL/NVDA

### Takeaway
Twelve Data, Alpha Vantage, and Finnhub all offer usable free tiers for historical daily OHLC on major US tickers, but each has meaningful caveats (Alpha Vantage: only 25 requests/day and 100-datapoint cap on the free "compact" output; Finnhub: 60 calls/min but limited depth of historical/fundamental data on free tier; Twelve Data: 800 calls/day but data delayed ~4 hours). Polygon.io's free tier is very rate-limited (5 req/min). Yahoo Finance's unofficial API was not independently re-verified in this research pass but is widely known/used as a free (unofficial, ToS-ambiguous) source for historical daily closes; it was not a subject of a direct source check here (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings
- **Alpha Vantage**: free tier is "limited to 25 requests per day with a rate limit of 5 per minute"; free users are capped at `outputsize=compact` (100 data points per request), while `outputsize=full` (20+ years of history) is "available to premium keys only" — [Source](https://dev.to/nexgendata/best-free-stock-market-apis-and-data-tools-in-2026-a-developers-honest-comparison-1926)
- **Finnhub**: free tier allows "60 requests per minute," but "does not include standardized financial statements, financials as reported, or key metrics" and "also limits historical data access on the free tier" — [Source](https://dev.to/nexgendata/best-free-stock-market-apis-and-data-tools-in-2026-a-developers-honest-comparison-1926)
- **Twelve Data**: free tier includes "800 API calls per day with four-hour delayed data for stocks" — [Source](https://dev.to/nexgendata/best-free-stock-market-apis-and-data-tools-in-2026-a-developers-honest-comparison-1926)
- **Polygon.io**: free API tier has "a call limit of 5 requests per minute" — [Source](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/clock-api-and-extended-hours/118)

### Inferences
- For a hackathon needing just a handful of specific historical daily closes (Friday close, Monday open) for 2–3 tickers (TSLA, AAPL, NVDA), any of Alpha Vantage, Twelve Data, or Finnhub's free tiers are more than sufficient in raw request volume — the real constraint is Alpha Vantage's 100-point/compact cap (fine for a few days of recent data, not for deep history) and Twelve Data's ~4-hour data delay (irrelevant for a Friday-close/Monday-open historical lookup, since that data is definitionally already finalized by the time it's queried).
- Twelve Data is likely the most practically convenient of the three for a quick MVP given its higher daily call ceiling (800/day) and straightforward historical-daily-OHLC endpoint, though Alpha Vantage is also extremely commonly used and free-tier friendly for a low-volume hackathon demo.

## Price freshness/staleness signals available on-chain or via API

### Takeaway
Three concrete, verifiable on-chain/off-chain signals exist and can be checked programmatically: (1) Chainlink's `latestRoundData()` returns an `updatedAt` Unix timestamp that a contract or off-chain agent can diff against `block.timestamp`/now to flag staleness; (2) Pyth's pull-oracle price updates carry a `publishTime` (and a confidence interval) per update, checkable the same way; (3) a DEX pool/pair's most recent swap event timestamp (queryable via DexScreener API fields like "last trade" / txn counts per time window, or directly via on-chain `Swap` event logs) serves as a proxy for "is this pool still trading" freshness.

### Cited Findings
- "Smart contracts should always check the `updatedAt` parameter returned from `latestRoundData()` and compare it to a staleness threshold. The staleness threshold should correspond to the heartbeat of the oracle's price feed" and "it is an error to assume that the same time interval heartbeat can be used as a staleness check for every feed, as different feeds can have different heartbeats" — [Source](https://github.com/Not-So-Smart-Contract/Chainlink-Oracle-Security-Considerations)
- "The aggregator updates its latestAnswer when the value deviates beyond a specified threshold or when the heartbeat idle time has passed... If your application detects that the reported answer is not updated within the heartbeat or within time limits that you determine are acceptable for your application, pause operation or switch to an alternate operation mode" — [Source](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds)
- Concretely for a stock feed: "a weekend freezes the [AAPL/USD Chainlink] feed for 48 to 58 hours; Labor Day stretched that past 81 hours" — demonstrating that `updatedAt`-based staleness checks are not just theoretical but produce very large, easily-detectable gaps for equity feeds specifically — [Source](https://github.com/moonwell-fi/mamo-contracts/pull/106)
- Pyth's pull-oracle price updates are time-stamped (`publishTime`) and confidence-scored at the point of publish, which is the mechanism by which a consuming contract determines freshness of a just-submitted update — [Source](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/pull-updates)
- Chainlink's newer 24/5 U.S. Equities Streams product explicitly markets itself as delivering "bid-ask data, last trade prices, volumes, market-status flags, and staleness indicators" as first-class payload fields — i.e., staleness indication is a built-in, explicit field in this product (availability on BSC unconfirmed, see above) — [Source](https://blog.chain.link/chainlink-24-5-us-equities-streams/)

### Inferences
- For this hackathon's exact use case (distinguishing a legitimately-stale-over-the-weekend "Friday close" price from a genuinely broken/dead feed), Chainlink's standard `updatedAt` staleness check is actually well-suited and requires no extra product: the team can define "fresh" as `updatedAt` within market hours, and explicitly treat a large gap that started right before a weekend as the intended "Friday close held over the weekend" behavior, while still flagging genuinely excessive gaps (e.g., >81 hours, per the Labor Day case) as a real problem.
- A DEX pool's on-chain `Swap` event timestamps (last-trade-time) are a legitimate, verifiable, and free additional freshness signal for the "wrapper liquidity" side of the product — i.e., even if the oracle price is fresh, the wrapper token itself might not be trading on PancakeSwap over the weekend, which DexScreener's per-pool data (recent txns/volume windows) or direct log queries can reveal.

### Gaps
- No public, standardized "staleness score" API/oracle was found that abstracts all of this into a single call — a hackathon team will need to implement the `updatedAt`/`publishTime`/last-swap-timestamp comparisons themselves (straightforward, but not off-the-shelf).

## Overall assessment: realistic MVP using ACTUAL BSC on-chain data vs. needing mocked/generic fallbacks

### Takeaway
A genuinely real-data MVP is realistic for most of the pipeline: real BEP-20 tokenized-stock contracts exist on BSC today (xStocks and Ondo Global Markets both confirmed live on BNB Chain), real DEX liquidity for them is queryable for free via DexScreener/DefiLlama, and real on-chain reference prices for at least AAPL/TSLA/NVDA are available from both Chainlink and Pyth directly on BSC. The one piece most likely to require a computed/self-built (not literally "mocked," but not a plug-and-play API either) component is "is the traditional market open right now" — there is no dedicated free on-chain oracle for this, so the team should compute it from NYSE's published hours+holiday calendar (free, reliable, simple) rather than search further for a nonexistent turnkey free API, or supplement with a rate-limited free API call (Alpaca/Finnhub) as a secondary check.

### Cited Findings
- **Real tokenized-stock contracts on BSC exist today**: "xStocks are deployed on BNB Chain as BEP-20 tokens by Backed... including assets like AAPLx, TSLAx, SPYx and NVDAx. In April 2026, xStocks went live on BNB Chain with 50+ tokenized US equities and ETFs" — [Source](https://blog.kraken.com/product/xstocks/bnb-chain-integration); [Source](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/bnb-chain-x-kraken-x-xstocks-trade-tokenized-equities-onchain-anytime)
- **Ondo Global Markets is also live on BNB Chain**: "Ondo Global Markets, the largest tokenized securities platform by total value locked (TVL), is now live on BNB Chain... now offers 430+ tokenized stocks and ETFs" — [Source](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/ondo-global-markets-brings-100-tokenized-u-s-stocks-etfs-to-bnb-chain"); [Source](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/10/29/ondo-brings-tokenized-u-s-stocks-to-bnb-chain-as-market-doubles-to-usd700m)
- Both xStocks and Ondo tokens "differ in structure and KYC rules... Ondo tokens are total return trackers: they reflect reinvested dividends rather than tracking the share price directly, which means the token price drifts from the underlying share price over time" — an important nuance for "reference price" design (Ondo's token price is not expected to equal the raw stock price) — [Source](https://www.bnbchain.org/en/blog/the-next-step-for-tokenized-equities-funding-paths-matter-after-assets-move-onchain)
- Real, free, on-chain reference prices for AAPL/TSLA/NVDA exist on BSC via both Chainlink (`data.chain.link/feeds/bsc/mainnet/...`) and Pyth (contract deployed on BNB Chain) — see citations above.
- Real, free DEX liquidity data for any BEP-20 pair (including these tokenized-stock tokens, once their pool addresses are identified) is available via DexScreener's free public API and DefiLlama's free API/PancakeSwap protocol page — see citations above.
- No dedicated free, always-on, on-chain or simple-API "is NYSE open right now" product was confirmed; the realistic free path is self-computing from NYSE's official calendar, optionally cross-checked with Alpaca's free `/v2/clock` or Finnhub's free US market-status endpoint — see citations above.

### Inferences
- The pieces that are **realistically available as genuine, live, free on-chain/API data today** (Sept 2026): (1) real tokenized-stock BEP-20 token contracts and identities (xStocks, Ondo GM); (2) real DEX liquidity/TVL for those tokens on PancakeSwap via DexScreener/DefiLlama; (3) a real on-chain reference price for at least AAPL, TSLA, NVDA via Chainlink and/or Pyth directly on BSC; (4) real historical Friday-close/Monday-open stock data via Twelve Data/Alpha Vantage/Finnhub free tiers; (5) a real, free, and simple-to-implement market-hours/holiday determination (self-computed from NYSE's official calendar).
- The one piece that is **not a turnkey free API** and must be engineered rather than fetched: "is the market open right now" as a single boolean — this is a minor calendar-math task, not a hard blocker, and should not require falling back to mocked data; it just isn't a single GET request to a dedicated oracle.
- A plausible reason a team might still choose to mock/generic-ify part of the pipeline is not data *unavailability* but **engineering time**: confirming exact BSC token/pool addresses for a specific tokenized ticker, wiring up both a Chainlink/Pyth read AND a DexScreener liquidity check AND a self-built market-calendar function, all in a hackathon timeframe, is real integration work across 3+ APIs/oracles — the data sources themselves are there, but a team should budget accordingly and could reasonably simplify by picking just one oracle (Chainlink, since it's simpler to read passively) and just one or two tickers (AAPL, TSLA — both confirmed present across xStocks, Ondo, Chainlink, and Pyth) rather than needing to mock the underlying numbers.

### Gaps
- Exact on-chain contract addresses for the specific xStocks/Ondo tokenized-stock BEP-20 tokens (e.g., the actual AAPLx or Ondo AAPL token address on BSC) and their PancakeSwap pool addresses were not looked up in this research pass — this is a necessary next step (via BscScan token search or the issuers' own docs — Backed's xStocks docs, Ondo's Global Markets docs) before the DexScreener liquidity lookups can be pointed at the right pairs.
- Whether Chainlink's or Pyth's AAPL/TSLA/NVDA feed price is actually the same "primary reference" the xStocks/Ondo tokens themselves are pegged/priced against (vs. simply a same-ticker but differently-sourced number) was not confirmed — worth checking each issuer's own stated oracle/pricing methodology (Backed's and Ondo's docs) to ensure the "reference price" and "wrapper price" being compared are conceptually aligned.
